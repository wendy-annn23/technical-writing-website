ABCs of Technical Writing

Discover the principles that help writers create accurate, organized, clear, and effective documents.

THE BIG IDEA

What Are the ABCs of Technical Writing?

The ABCs of Technical Writing are principles that guide writers in preparing clear, accurate, organized, and professional documents. They help writers communicate information effectively and make reports easier for readers to understand.

These principles cover accuracy, brevity, confidence, dignity, emphasis, facility, grammatical correctness, honesty, illustration, judgment, knowledge, logic, mechanical neatness, normal procedure, objectivity, planning, qualification, revision, straight sentences, thoroughness, unity, viewpoint, word choice, extra effort, you-point, and zest.

VISUAL GUIDE

Important Principles in Practice

Technical writing combines correct information, clear organization, and appropriate visuals. Here are some examples of how these principles can be applied.

Accuracy

Check facts, figures, and data before including them in a report.

Mechanical Neatness

Present information using consistent formatting and an organized layout.

Revision

Review spelling, punctuation, statements, and the overall content before submitting.

A–Z LEARNING GUIDE

Alphabet of Technical Writing

Review each letter to understand the principles and their meanings. The definitions below use simplified wording to make them easier to study.

A

Accuracy

Tactfulness in recording data; record information correctly and carefully.

B

Brevity

Being direct to the point and expressing ideas concisely.

C

Confidence

Being decisive in communicating information.

D

Dignity

Showing courtesy to readers and maintaining a professional tone.

E

Emphasis

Knowing what is important to the reader and highlighting it appropriately.

F

Facility

Using devices and techniques that make a report easy to understand.

G

Grammatical Correctness

Using correct grammar, spelling, punctuation, and sentence construction.

H

Honesty

Citing sources and giving proper credit for borrowed information.

I

Illustration

Using charts, graphs, and diagrams to explain information.

J

Judgment

Qualifying data and evaluating gathered information carefully.

K

Knowledge

Building an appropriate collection of data and information about the subject.

L

Logic

Organizing ideas through logical reasoning and a sensible sequence.

M

Mechanical Neatness

Maintaining the general appearance and neatness of a report.

N

Normal Procedure

Following accepted procedures and conforming to general standards.

O

Objectivity

Considering the reader's perspective and presenting information fairly.

P

Planning

Giving purpose and direction to the writing process.

Q

Qualification

Evaluating ideas to determine which information should be included.

R

Revision

Checking statements, spelling, punctuation, and other details.

S

Straight Sentence

Using appropriate sentence structures and transitional devices to connect ideas.

T

Thoroughness

Providing the necessary substance and details of a report.

U

Unity

Maintaining coherence and connection among ideas.

V

Viewpoint

Considering the perspective from which information is written.

W

Word Choice

Avoiding words that are difficult to understand and choosing suitable terms.

X

Extra Effort

Taking additional care to improve the quality and completeness of a report.

Y

You-Point

Considering the reader's needs and making information useful to them.

Z

Zest

Writing in a lively and engaging way while keeping information clear and professional.

Remember this ✿

Effective technical writing requires more than correct grammar. Writers should organize ideas, verify facts, consider their readers, use suitable illustrations, and revise their work carefully.
